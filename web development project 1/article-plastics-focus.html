In Focus: Plastics

Published: November 2025 7 min read

When was the last time you bought something that was packaged with plastic? Chances are that it was the last time you bought anything, period.

The Ubiquity of Plastic Packaging

Plastic has become so deeply integrated into our daily lives that it's nearly impossible to go through a single day without encountering it. From food containers to shipping materials, electronics to cosmetics, plastic packaging is everywhere. This omnipresence is both a testament to plastic's versatility and a warning sign of our over-reliance on this material.

Why Plastic Became Dominant

Understanding why plastic packaging became so prevalent helps us address the challenge of replacing it:

  • Low Cost: Plastic is cheap to produce and transport
  • Versatility: Can be molded into any shape and size
  • Durability: Protects products effectively during shipping
  • Lightweight: Reduces shipping costs and fuel consumption
  • Barrier Properties: Keeps food fresh and extends shelf life

The Environmental Crisis

However, the very properties that make plastic packaging so useful also make it an environmental nightmare:

Ocean Pollution

An estimated 8 million tons of plastic waste enters our oceans every year. By 2050, there could be more plastic than fish in the ocean by weight. Marine animals mistake plastic for food, leading to injury and death across countless species.

Microplastics

As plastic breaks down, it doesn't disappear—it fragments into smaller and smaller pieces called microplastics. These particles have been found everywhere from the deepest ocean trenches to Arctic ice, and even in human blood and organs.

Carbon Emissions

Plastic production is responsible for significant greenhouse gas emissions. If plastic production continues at current rates, it could account for 15% of the global carbon budget by 2050.

Single-Use Plastics: The Biggest Culprit

Single-use plastics—items used once and then discarded—represent the largest portion of plastic waste. Common examples include:

  • Plastic bags and wraps
  • Food containers and clamshells
  • Disposable cutlery and straws
  • Bubble wrap and packing peanuts
  • Plastic bottles and caps

Alternatives to Traditional Plastic Packaging

1. Biodegradable Plastics

Made from plant-based materials like corn starch or sugarcane, these plastics can break down naturally under the right conditions. However, many require industrial composting facilities and won't degrade in natural environments.

2. Paper and Cardboard

Traditional materials making a comeback. Modern innovations have made paper-based packaging more durable and moisture-resistant without plastic coatings.

3. Glass and Metal

Infinitely recyclable materials that can be reused without degradation. While heavier than plastic, their environmental benefits often outweigh the transportation costs.

4. Innovative Bio-Materials

New materials like mushroom packaging, seaweed films, and agricultural waste-based packaging offer exciting plastic-free alternatives.

What Businesses Can Do

Transitioning away from plastic packaging requires commitment but offers significant benefits:

  • Conduct a packaging audit to identify plastic use
  • Prioritize eliminating single-use plastics first
  • Partner with suppliers offering plastic-free alternatives
  • Educate customers about proper disposal methods
  • Set measurable reduction targets and track progress
  • Consider reusable packaging systems where feasible

The Path Forward

While plastic packaging won't disappear overnight, every step toward reduction matters. By choosing sustainable alternatives, businesses can reduce their environmental impact while meeting growing consumer demand for eco-friendly practices.
